## Infrastructure & Outage Exposure

Avalanche's infrastructure pillar is pulled down by meaningful AWS concentration (~40% exposure). Its strong point is a high Nakamoto Coefficient for infrastructure (28) from ~1,700 validators, so its distribution is broad even though it leans on hyperscalers for hosting.

## What the Score Means

- **Infrastructure (62):** good validator count, but AWS reliance reduces single-outage resilience.
- **Capital (55):** foundation and insider unlocks retain notable influence over token economics.
- **Software (52):** a single dominant client (AvalancheGo) with coreth further back, limiting diversity.

Avalanche has climbed from ~38 (2020) to ~60 (2025). Track the history in the [Terminal](https://web3decentralization.com/terminal/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Avalanche decentralized?

Reasonably — it has ~1,700 validators and a flexible subnet architecture, but depends on AWS and a dominant client, which lowers its score.

### What makes Avalanche different on decentralization?

Its subnet sovereignty model lets app-chains control their own validators, a unique design; the base primary network still carries the concentration risk.

### Is Avalanche more decentralized than Solana?

Yes, slightly (57.2 vs 53.2) thanks to better validator distribution and lower capital concentration, though Solana edges it on client work.

## Final Verdict

Avalanche offers unique subnet-driven decentralization but carries meaningful AWS and single-client concentration. It sits mid-tier, better than Solana or XRP, below Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Polkadot.
