## Infrastructure & Outage Exposure

XRP Ledger leans on a curated unique node list (UNL) of ~120 validators that Ripple and the XRPL Foundation influence. Its hyperscaler exposure is the lowest in the set (~25% AWS), but the federated design means governance is gated by a trusted validators set rather than open participation.

## What the Score Means

- **Capital (35):** the lowest pillar — Ripple escrow holds ~40 billion XRP, a very high concentration relative to supply.
- **Governance (40):** protocol upgrades are effectively gated by the curated validator UNL.
- **Infrastructure (45):** a small, curated validator set makes the network performant but not broadly permissionless.

XRP's score has stayed in the 30s–40s since 2012, reflecting its structurally permissioned design rather than a recent regression. See the timeline in the [Terminal](https://web3decentralization.com/terminal/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is XRP decentralized?

To a limited degree. It is performant and federated, but its validator UNL is curated by Ripple/the XRPL Foundation and token supply is highly concentrated, which caps its score.

### Why is XRP's decentralization score lowest among the audited chains?

Three linked factors: a curated validator list, ~40B XRP held in escrow, and a single dominant client.

### Can XRP Ledger become more decentralized?

Yes, if the UNL grows in size and independent diversity, and if escrow release reduces token concentration — both are watched metrics in the Terminal.

## Final Verdict

XRP Ledger optimizes for throughput and cross-border settlement rather than permissionless decentralization. Its curated validator structure and token concentration produce the lowest composite in this audit — a deliberate design trade-off, not an accident.
