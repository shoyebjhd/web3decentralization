## Infrastructure & Outage Exposure

Cosmos Hub's architecture relies on a ~180-validator set, which is individually manageable but concentrates voting power among top CEX and institutional stakers. Its hyperscaler exposure is moderate (about 27% AWS), with a bare-metal-heavy operator base that reduces single-host reliance.

## What the Score Means

- **Governance (78):** battle-tested on-chain governance via the Cosmos SDK is genuinely active and functional.
- **Infrastructure (70):** a smaller validator cap concentrates infrastructure relative to Polkadot or Cardano.
- **Capital (58):** top CEX and institutional stakers hold material voting power — the key centralization vector.

Cosmos Hub rose from ~42 (2019) to ~69 (2025), aided by IBC expansion and replicated security. See the timeline in the [Terminal](https://web3decentralization.com/terminal/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Cosmos decentralized?

Reasonably — it has strong on-chain governance and a bare-metal operator base, but a smaller validator cap and concentrated CEX staking keep its composite mid-tier.

### What is Cosmos's main decentralization risk?

Capital concentration among top stakers (exchanges and institutions) who can influence governance via voting power.

### Why does Cosmos use Gaia?

Gaia is the reference implementation of the Cosmos SDK + CometBFT; its single dominance is a soft spot for client diversity.

## Final Verdict

Cosmos Hub is a solid mid-tier network with active governance and a genuine bare-metal operator base, but a smaller validator cap and concentrated staking keep it below the top tier on decentralization.
