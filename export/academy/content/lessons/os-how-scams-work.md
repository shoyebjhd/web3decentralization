Scams don't break cryptography — they break *you*, at the exact moment something valuable is one click away. This lesson dissects the five working scams of 2026 so you recognize the machinery, not just the costume.

## 1. Phishing sites (the volume leader)

A pixel-perfect copy of a wallet, exchange, or dApp. You "connect" or "log in," approve one malicious transaction, and your wallet drains. Delivery: search ads, DMs, QR codes in public, typosquatted domains. **Defense:** type domains yourself; bookmark real ones; treat every link as hostile.

## 2. Fake support (the trust exploit)

"You have a stuck transaction / verification / bonus — our agent will help." Real support never DMs first, never asks for keys, never needs a "refundable deposit." Urgency + authority + a small upfront payment = the formula. **Defense:** initiate all support contact yourself, on official channels.

## 3. Fake airdrops & giveaways (the greed hook)

"Connect to claim 500 tokens." The site drains on connect, or the tokens are [honeypots](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/honeypot/) you can never sell. Celebrity-doubling scams are the same shape. **Defense:** real [airdrops](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/airdrop/) never require connecting, paying, or hurrying.

## 4. Rug pulls (the insider exit)

Devs launch a token, hype it, then drain [liquidity](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/liquidity/). Red flags: anonymous team + huge insider allocation + unlocked liquidity + "can't-miss" marketing. **Defense:** check holders, locks, and contract verification first. If you can't find who controls the money, assume it's the scammer.

## 5. Clipboard malware & fake apps (the technical ones)

Malware swaps pasted addresses; fake wallet apps in app stores steal seeds at import. **Defense:** verify pasted addresses character-by-character; download wallets only from official domains; use hardware signing for size.

## The universal detector

Every scam answers YES to at least two of: *Did they contact me first? Is it urgent? Does it need my keys, an approval, or an upfront payment?* Two yeses — walk away, verify elsewhere, come back tomorrow.

> Scammers sell certainty on a deadline. Safety is the habit of pausing.
