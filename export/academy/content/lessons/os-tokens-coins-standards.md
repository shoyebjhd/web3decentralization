A **coin** powers its own chain (BTC, ETH, SOL) — it pays for security and fees. A **token** lives on someone else's chain as a [smart contract](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/smart-contract/) (USDT on Ethereum, UNI, game items). Standards like [ERC-20](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/erc-20/) make tokens interoperable: build once, work in every wallet and exchange.

## The token zoo

- **Payment coins** (BTC, LTC): money-first, simplest thesis. - **Platform coins** (ETH, SOL): fuel for computation; demand tracks usage. - **Stablecoins** (USDC, DAI): pegged dollars; differ by backing (see the   [DeFi 101 path](https://web3decentralization.com/course/defi-101/)). - **Governance tokens** ([UNI, ARB](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/governance-token/)): votes with   a price tag — and whale-concentration caveats. - **NFTs** ([ERC-721](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/nft/)): unique items, not interchangeable money. - **Wrapped assets** ([WBTC](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/wrapped-token/)): IOUs for   cross-chain use, custodian risk included.

## Reading any token in 60 seconds

1. Coin or token? Which chain secures it? 2. [Max supply](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/max-supply/) vs [circulating](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/circulating-supply/)    vs unlock schedule ([vesting](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/vesting/))? 3. What is it *for* — fee fuel, votes, access, or pure speculation? 4. Who holds the big wallets (explorers don't lie)?

## Why standards matter more than tokens

ERC-20 did for assets what the web's HTTP did for pages: one interface, infinite compatible tools. New standards (NFTs, soulbound, tokenized real-world assets) follow the same playbook — the standard, not any single token, is the durable innovation.

> Tokens are easy to create and hard to value. The standard tells you the
> > mechanics; the distribution tells you the truth.
