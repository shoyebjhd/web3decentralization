Transfers are permanent, so professionals use a ritual: verify, test, send. This lesson turns that ritual into muscle memory.

## Receiving

1. In your wallet, tap Receive and select the **exact asset + network**    (USDT on Ethereum vs USDT on Tron are different destinations). 2. Copy the [address](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/address/) — or show your QR code in person. 3. Share it freely; addresses are public by design. 4. Watch the transfer confirm on a [block explorer](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/block-explorer/).

## Sending — the five-point check

1. **Address**: paste, then compare first + last 6 characters with the    recipient's message. Malware rewrites clipboard addresses. 2. **Network**: must match what the recipient expects. Wrong network is the    #1 cause of "lost" funds. 3. **Asset**: sending the wrong token to a contract or exchange memo-less    account can strand it. 4. **Fee**: sane for the network right now? Absurd fees mean a misclick —    cancel and re-check. 5. **Test amount**: for any new destination or large sum, send a tiny probe    first, confirm arrival, then send the rest.

## Fees and timing, practically

- Check current [gas](https://web3decentralization.com/glossary/gas/) before urgent sends; non-urgent ones   can wait for quiet hours. - Bitcoin needs confirmations (10+ min typical); Ethereum ~seconds to minutes;   always check the explorer rather than trusting "sent" screens. - A "pending" transaction isn't failed — don't re-send blindly and double-pay.

## The mistake board (learn from others)

- Wrong network → usually unrecoverable without the recipient's cooperation.
- Typo'd address → gone. Checksums catch most, not all.
- Sent to a scammer → gone. No reversals exist. Ever.

> Verify twice, test once, send once. Boring transfers are successful
> > transfers.
