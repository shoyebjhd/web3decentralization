What is Crypto Staking?

Crypto staking is the process of locking up a certain amount of cryptocurrency to participate in the validation and security of a blockchain network that uses a Proof of Stake (PoS) consensus mechanism. In return for committing your assets, you earn staking rewards — typically paid in the same cryptocurrency you staked.

Think of it like earning interest in a savings account, except your funds help operate a decentralized network instead of sitting in a bank vault. Staking serves a critical purpose: it helps verify transactions, produce new blocks, and keep the network secure against attacks.

The concept gained mainstream attention after Ethereum completed its transition from Proof of Work to Proof of Stake in September 2022 — an event known as "The Merge." Since then, millions of ETH holders have staked their coins, and the broader ecosystem of staking options has exploded. In 2026, how to stake crypto is a question with more answers — and better tools — than ever before.

How Staking Works

At its core, staking is the engine that powers Proof of Stake blockchains. Here is a simplified breakdown of the process:

Proof of Stake Consensus

In a PoS network, validators are chosen to create new blocks and confirm transactions based on the amount of cryptocurrency they have staked — essentially locked as collateral. The more coins a validator holds (or is delegated by others), the higher the chance they are selected to validate a block.

Validators vs. Delegators

Validators are nodes that run the software to process transactions and produce blocks. Running a validator typically requires technical knowledge and a minimum stake (for example, 32 ETH on Ethereum). Delegators are everyday users who assign their coins to a validator. You do not need to run any hardware — you simply stake through a wallet or exchange and share in the validator's rewards.

Earning Rewards

When a validator successfully proposes and attests to a block, the network issues new tokens as a reward. These rewards are distributed proportionally to everyone who staked with that validator. Rewards vary by network and can fluctuate based on factors like total network participation, inflation rate, and validator performance.

Liquid Staking

A major innovation in recent years is liquid staking. Platforms like Lido and Rocket Pool let you stake your ETH (or other tokens) and receive a liquid staking token in return — for example, stETH. This token represents your staked position and can be traded, lent, or used in DeFi while your original coins remain staked. Liquid staking solves the liquidity problem that traditionally came with locking up assets.

Best Cryptocurrencies to Stake in 2026

Not all staking opportunities are equal. Here are the top cryptocurrencies to consider staking in 2026, along with their typical APY and key details.

Ethereum (ETH)

Ethereum remains the king of staking by total value locked. With hundreds of billions of dollars staked, ETH offers a typical APY of 3% to 4%. Ethereum's massive ecosystem of DeFi protocols, NFTs, and layer-2 networks makes it a blue-chip choice. You can stake as little as a fraction of an ETH through exchanges or liquid staking protocols — no need for the full 32 ETH validator requirement.

Solana (SOL)

Solana is known for its high throughput and low transaction costs. Staking SOL typically yields 6% to 8% APY. Solana's staking model is straightforward: you delegate your SOL to a validator through the Phantom wallet or other compatible wallets. There is no lockup period on Solana, meaning you can unstake at any time — a significant flexibility advantage.

Cardano (ADA)

Cardano takes a research-driven approach to blockchain development. Staking ADA is simple and accessible, with typical APY around 3% to 5%. Cardano uses a unique delegation system where staking pools handle validation. There are no lockup periods, and your ADA always remains in your wallet even when staked — one of the most user-friendly staking experiences in crypto.

Polkadot (DOT)

Polkadot enables interoperability between different blockchains. Staking DOT offers one of the higher yields in the space, typically 10% to 14% APY. However, Polkadot uses a nominated Proof of Stake system with a 28-day unbonding period. You must choose your nominators carefully, as underperforming validators can result in reduced rewards.

Cosmos (ATOM)

The Cosmos ecosystem powers an ever-growing network of interconnected blockchains. Staking ATOM yields roughly 7% to 15% APY, depending on the validator. Cosmos has a 21-day unbonding period. The ecosystem also offers opportunities to earn airdrops from new projects launching in the Cosmos SDK, providing additional upside for stakers.

Where to Stake Crypto

There are three main ways to stake your cryptocurrency, each with its own tradeoffs.

Centralized Exchange Staking

The easiest entry point for beginners is through a centralized exchange. Platforms like Binance staking and Bybit staking allow you to stake popular coins with just a few clicks. The advantages are simplicity, beginner-friendly interfaces, and often competitive APY rates. The downside is that you are trusting a third party with your keys — you do not hold the coins yourself during the staking period.

Native Wallet Staking

For those who want to maintain control of their private keys, staking through a native wallet is the preferred method. For Ethereum, you can use MetaMask with a liquid staking protocol. For Solana, the Phantom wallet offers built-in staking. Cardano users can stake through the Daedalus or Yoroi wallets. This method keeps your coins in your own wallet while still earning rewards.

Decentralized Finance (DeFi) Staking

DeFi staking involves using protocols like Lido, Rocket Pool, or Jito to stake your assets in a decentralized manner. This often comes with the added benefit of liquid staking tokens. While this approach is more aligned with crypto's decentralized ethos, it introduces smart contract risk — if a protocol is exploited, your funds could be at risk.

Step-by-Step: How to Stake on an Exchange

If you are new to crypto staking, here is a simple walkthrough for staking through a centralized exchange:

  1. Create and verify an account. Sign up on a reputable exchange that offers staking. Complete the KYC (Know Your Customer) verification process with your ID and personal details.
  2. Deposit or buy crypto. Fund your account by depositing cryptocurrency from another wallet or by purchasing it directly with fiat currency (USD, EUR, etc.) using a bank transfer or card.
  3. Navigate to the staking section. Look for the "Earn," "Staking," or "DeFi Earn" tab in the exchange's menu. This is where staking products are listed.
  4. Choose your coin and staking plan. Select the cryptocurrency you want to stake. Many exchanges offer flexible, 30-day, 60-day, and 90-day lockup options — longer lockups typically offer higher APY.
  5. Review the terms. Check the APY, lockup period, minimum staking amount, and any fees. Make sure you are comfortable with the commitment before proceeding.
  6. Confirm and stake. Enter the amount you want to stake, review the summary, and confirm. Your coins will be locked for the chosen period, and rewards will begin accruing.
  7. Monitor your earnings. Check your staking dashboard regularly to track rewards. Most exchanges show your estimated APY, rewards earned, and unlock date in one place.

Staking Risks to Consider

While staking is generally considered one of the safer ways to earn passive income in crypto, it is not without risks. Understanding these risks is essential before you commit your funds.

Lockup Periods

Many staking options require your coins to be locked for a set period — anywhere from a few days to 28 days or more. During this time, you cannot sell or transfer your staked crypto. If the market drops sharply, you may be unable to exit your position. Networks like Solana and Cardano offer no lockup, which provides more flexibility.

Slashing

Slashing is a penalty mechanism used by some Proof of Stake networks. If a validator acts maliciously or goes offline for extended periods, a portion of their staked coins — and the coins delegated to them — can be burned. While reputable exchanges and validators minimize this risk, it is important to choose your validator carefully.

Price Volatility

Even if you earn 8% APY, a 30% drop in the token's price would far outweigh your staking rewards. Crypto markets remain highly volatile, and staking does not protect you from market downturns. Only stake what you can afford to hold long-term.

Smart Contract Risk

If you stake through DeFi protocols, your funds are subject to smart contract risk. Bugs or vulnerabilities in the code could be exploited by hackers. Always use audited, well-established protocols and consider diversifying across multiple platforms.

Platform Risk

When staking on a centralized exchange, you are trusting the platform to safeguard your assets. History has shown that exchanges can fail or become insolvent. For significant amounts, consider self-custody staking through a hardware wallet and native protocol.

Staking vs. Other Passive Income Strategies

Staking is not the only way to earn yield on your crypto. Here is how it compares to other popular strategies:

Crypto Lending

Crypto lending involves depositing your coins into a platform that lends them to borrowers and pays you interest. Lending can offer competitive APY, sometimes higher than staking, but it introduces counterparty risk — if the borrower or platform defaults, you could lose your funds. Post-2022, the crypto lending landscape has become more regulated but still carries more risk than native staking.

Yield Farming

Yield farming involves providing liquidity to decentralized exchanges (DEXs) and earning trading fees plus token incentives. While yields can be significantly higher than staking, yield farming comes with impermanent loss — the risk that the value of your deposited tokens changes compared to simply holding them. It is a more advanced strategy better suited to experienced DeFi users.

How Staking Compares

For most beginners and long-term holders, crypto staking offers the best balance of simplicity, reliability, and risk-adjusted returns. You do not need to manage complex liquidity positions or worry about counterparty risk from lending platforms. Staking lets you earn rewards while directly supporting the network you believe in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is crypto staking safe?

Crypto staking is relatively safe compared to other yield strategies, but it is not risk-free. The main risks are price volatility, lockup periods, and (for DeFi staking) smart contract vulnerabilities. Using reputable platforms and staking through well-audited protocols significantly reduces risk.

How much money do I need to start staking?

It depends on the platform and the coin. Many exchanges have very low minimums — you can start staking ETH or SOL with as little as $10 worth of crypto. Some networks like Polkadot may have higher minimums for direct validator staking, but delegation through exchanges lowers that barrier.

Do I have to pay taxes on staking rewards?

In most jurisdictions, staking rewards are considered taxable income at the time you receive them. Tax treatment varies by country, so it is important to consult a tax professional familiar with cryptocurrency regulations in your region.

What is liquid staking?

Liquid staking lets you stake your crypto while receiving a tradable token (like stETH) that represents your staked position. This means you can use your staked assets in DeFi — lending, trading, or providing liquidity — while still earning staking rewards. It solves the liquidity problem of traditional staking.

Can I unstake my crypto anytime?

It depends on the network and platform. Solana and Cardano allow instant unstaking. Ethereum through liquid staking protocols allows you to sell your liquid staking token at any time. However, traditional staking on networks like Polkadot and Cosmos has unbonding periods of 28 and 21 days respectively.

What is the difference between staking and mining?

Mining is used in Proof of Work networks like Bitcoin, where computers solve complex puzzles to validate transactions. Staking is used in Proof of Stake networks, where validators are chosen based on the amount of crypto they hold. Staking is far more energy-efficient and accessible to everyday users.
