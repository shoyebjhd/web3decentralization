What Is MEXC?

MEXC (MEXC Global) is a centralized exchange known primarily as a go-to destination for a massive catalogue of altcoins — it lists a very large number of tokens, frequently onboarding early-stage and niche projects well before larger venues. It pairs that breadth with aggressive, low fee structures and a tier model (MEXC Points / MX token) that rewards holders.

Trading Fees

MEXC competes hard on cost. It runs low base maker/taker fees — around the 0.00%/0.10% range in many cases — and adds maker rebates in some tiers, meaning active market makers can effectively trade for free or even get paid. Holding MX can further reduce fees. This is among the most aggressive fee structures in the industry, beating even the biggest global exchanges on price for frequent traders.

The Altcoin Catalogue (MEXC's Moat)

MEXC's biggest draw is sheer token availability. It regularly lists hundreds of additional coins versus major rivals, making it the practical venue for early access to new, small-cap and niche tokens. The trade-off: many of those tokens are high-risk micro-caps where liquidity and legitimacy vary sharply. This is where seasoned-position traders find alpha — and where impulsive beginners get burned if they chase "the next big listing" without research.

Security and Trust

MEXC publishes Proof of Reserves and uses cold storage. However, like all altcoin-heavy platforms, its over-the-counter and new-listing focus means users must be far more careful about token quality and liquidity than on a conservative exchange such as Kraken. Withdraw any holdings you cannot afford to lose, and treat new micro-cap listings as high-risk speculation.
