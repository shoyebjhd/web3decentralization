What is Binance?

Binance is a centralized cryptocurrency exchange (CEX) founded in 2017 by Changpeng Zhao (CZ) and Yi He. What started as a modest ICO-funded startup quickly became the dominant force in global crypto trading. Today, Binance is the most liquid and most trafficked exchange on the planet, serving over 200 million registered users across more than 180 countries.

At its core, Binance allows users to buy, sell, and trade digital assets — from Bitcoin and Ethereum to thousands of smaller altcoins that rarely appear on competing platforms. Beyond simple spot trading, Binance has evolved into a full-service crypto ecosystem offering derivatives, staking, earning products, a decentralized exchange (Binance DEX / BNB Chain), an NFT marketplace, a Launchpad for new token offerings, and its own blockchain infrastructure.

The exchange operates under multiple regulatory entities globally, with its primary international hub based in Dubai. U.S. users access a separate, compliant platform through Binance.US. Despite regulatory scrutiny in 2023, Binance has invested heavily in compliance infrastructure, hiring former regulators and implementing robust KYC and AML programs.

Binance Fees

Fee structure is one of the most common topics in any Binance review, and for good reason — Binance is consistently among the cheapest exchanges in the industry.

Spot Trading Fees

Binance's base spot trading fee is 0.10% for both makers and takers. This is already lower than most competitors. However, fees can be reduced further through two mechanisms:

  • BNB Discount: Holding and using BNB to pay fees earns an automatic 25% discount, bringing the base rate down to 0.075%.
  • VIP Tiers: Based on 30-day trading volume and BNB holdings, users can unlock VIP levels 1 through 9, with fees dropping as low as 0.012% maker / 0.024% taker at the highest tier.

Futures Trading Fees

Binance Futures charges a 0.02% maker / 0.04% taker fee on USDT-margined perpetual and delivery contracts at the standard tier. VIP users pay significantly less, and high-frequency traders can negotiate custom fee structures through Binance's institutional desk.

Withdrawal and Deposit Fees

Binance does not charge fees for cryptocurrency deposits. Withdrawal fees vary by asset and network; for example, a BTC withdrawal on the Bitcoin network typically costs 0.0001–0.0005 BTC depending on network congestion. Fiat withdrawal fees depend on the payment method and currency, ranging from free (for certain bank transfers) to 1–2% for card withdrawals.

Binance Security

Security is the question every prospective user asks in a Binance review, and it's a topic with nuance. On the technical side, Binance has a strong track record. On the regulatory side, the picture is more complex.

Technical Security

  • SAFU Fund: Binance established the Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU) in 2018, funded with 10% of all trading fees. The fund held over $1 billion at its peak and is designed to reimburse users in the event of a security breach.
  • Cold Storage: The vast majority of user funds (reportedly 95%+) are held in cold, air-gapped wallets, minimizing exposure to online attacks.
  • Multi-Factor Authentication: Binance supports Google Authenticator, SMS 2FA, hardware key (YubiKey), and biometric login. The platform also offers withdrawal address whitelisting, anti-phishing codes, and device management.
  • Proof of Reserves: Since late 2022, Binance publishes monthly Merkle-tree proofs of reserves, demonstrating that customer balances are fully backed 1:1 (and often above 100%).

Track Record and Regulatory Events

Binance has never been hacked in a way that resulted in a direct loss of user funds from hot wallets. However, in November 2023, the exchange and CZ reached a settlement with U.S. regulators. Binance agreed to pay $4.3 billion to resolve charges from the DOJ, FinCEN, and OFAC related to anti-money-laundering compliance failures. CZ stepped down as CEO and was sentenced to four months in prison. Richard Teng, a former regulatory executive, succeeded CZ as CEO.

Since the settlement, Binance has restructured its compliance division, partnered with Chainalysis for enhanced on-chain monitoring, and cooperated with regulators in jurisdictions including France, Dubai, and Japan. The exchange maintains its position that user assets have always been fully backed.

Spot and Margin Trading

Futures and Derivatives

Binance Earn

Binance Earn is a suite of yield-generating products including:

  • Simple Earn: Flexible and locked savings accounts offering variable or fixed APY on major tokens.
  • Staking: Delegated proof-of-stake staking for ETH, SOL, ADA, and dozens of other PoS assets.
  • Liquidity Farming and BNB Vault: Yield aggregation products for passive income.

Launchpad

Binance Launchpad is the exchange's token launch platform for new projects. Users can commit BNB to participate in Initial Exchange Offerings (IEOs). Historically, Launchpad has delivered strong returns for participants, though past performance does not guarantee future results.

P2P Trading

Binance P2P allows users to buy and sell crypto directly with each other using local payment methods in 100+ countries. It supports major fiat currencies and is especially popular in regions with limited banking access.

NFT Marketplace

The Binance NFT marketplace supports minting, buying, and selling NFTs on the BNB Chain. It offers lower gas fees compared to Ethereum-based marketplaces and has featured collections from major entertainment and gaming brands.

Binance Card

The Binance Visa Card lets users spend crypto at over 60 million merchants worldwide, with up to 8% cashback in BNB depending on card tier. No conversion fees apply when spending supported assets.

Who Should Use Binance?

Binance is best suited for intermediate to advanced crypto traders who prioritize low fees, deep liquidity, and access to a vast range of altcoins. Day traders and scalpers will appreciate the thin order books and low taker fees on both spot and futures markets. Derivatives traders benefit from the sheer size and liquidity of Binance Futures, which consistently ranks as the top derivatives exchange by open interest.

Passive investors and HODLers can also find value in Binance Earn, which provides competitive staking and savings rates. The Launchpad is an attractive feature for users looking to participate in early-stage token sales.

Complete beginners may find the full Binance interface overwhelming at first, though the "Binance Convert" feature and simplified mobile interface help lower the barrier to entry. If you want a beginner-friendly experience with strong regulatory standing in the U.S., Coinbase may be a simpler starting point — but you will pay significantly higher fees for the privilege.

Is Binance Legal and Safe in 2026?

Binance operates legally in the vast majority of countries worldwide, though availability varies by jurisdiction. As of 2026, Binance holds regulatory licenses or registrations in key markets including:

  • UAE (Dubai): Fully licensed under VARA (Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority)
  • France: Registered as a Digital Asset Service Provider (DASP)
  • Japan: Registered as a crypto exchange with the FSA
  • Australia: Registered with AUSTRAC

Users in the United States should use Binance.US, which is a separate entity that operates under U.S. regulatory requirements. Binance.US supports a smaller selection of coins and lacks some advanced features like high-leverage futures.

Some countries have fully restricted Binance access, including (as of early 2026) the United Kingdom, Ontario (Canada), and Singapore. Users should always check local regulations before signing up.

Regarding safety, Binance has not experienced a breach of user hot wallets and maintains the SAFU insurance fund, cold storage for the majority of assets, and monthly proof-of-reserves audits. The 2023 U.S. regulatory settlement was a serious event, but the exchange has since undertaken significant compliance overhauls under new leadership.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Binance safe to use in 2026?

Binance maintains strong technical security with cold storage, the SAFU insurance fund, and monthly proof-of-reserves reports. For most users, Binance is a safe platform, though you should always use strong 2FA and enable withdrawal whitelisting.

How much are Binance fees?

Base spot fees are 0.10% for makers and takers. Using BNB for fee payments reduces this by 25% to 0.075%. VIP users with high trading volumes can pay as low as 0.012% maker. Futures fees start at 0.02% maker / 0.04% taker.

Can I use Binance in the United States?

Yes, but through Binance.US, which is a separate, regulated platform with a more limited feature set and coin selection than the global Binance exchange.

What is the minimum to trade on Binance?

There is no strict minimum deposit for crypto. For spot trades, the minimum order size is typically equivalent to $5–$10 depending on the trading pair. Futures contracts have slightly higher minimums.

Does Binance support staking?

Yes. Binance Earn offers flexible and locked staking for dozens of proof-of-stake cryptocurrencies, with variable APYs that are generally competitive with dedicated staking platforms.

How does Binance make money?

Binance generates revenue primarily through trading fees on spot and derivatives markets, withdrawal fees, listing fees from new projects, interest on margin lending, and commissions from Binance Card transactions.

What happened with CZ and Binance in 2023?

In November 2023, Binance and its founder Changpeng Zhao (CZ) settled with U.S. regulators. Binance agreed to a $4.3 billion payment to resolve charges related to anti-money-laundering violations.
