What Is Kraken?

Founded in 2011, Kraken is one of the crypto industry's oldest and most battle-tested exchanges. It survived the 2017 cycle, the 2020–21 boom, and the 2022 collapse without losing customers' funds. It is known for a rigorous, security-first culture and higher regulatory compliance relative to global competitors.

Trading Fees

Kraken's standard interface has relatively high fees (for convenience). Most serious traders use Kraken Pro (free), where maker/taker starts around 0.16%/0.26% and falls with volume — competitive, though not the absolute lowest in the industry. If fees are your top priority, Binance and Bybit undercut Kraken; if trust and compliance matter more, Kraken's premium is defensible.

Security and Trust — Kraken's Moat

Kraken's selling point is genuine. It has never suffered a hack that resulted in customers losing funds in its 15-year history, runs a renowned security team, keeps the vast majority of assets in cold storage, and publishes proof-of-reserve attestations from an independent auditor. For users whose first concern is "will my money be safe with this company for years," Kraken is the benchmark among major exchanges.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Kraken safe?

Yes — arguably the safest major exchange. It has never lost customer funds to a hack, uses extensive cold storage, and publishes audited reserve attestations. No platform is risk-free, but Kraken is the security benchmark.

Is Kraken cheaper than Binance?

No, generally not — Binance's fees are lower. But Kraken Pro's fees are competitive, and for many users the security/regulatory premium is worth the small extra cost.

Is Kraken good for beginners?

Reasonably — the standard interface is clean and straightforward, though fees are higher at base level. It is a sensible and simple venue for buying and holding Bitcoin.
