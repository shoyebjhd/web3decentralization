What Makes an Exchange Good for Buying Bitcoin?

"Best" depends on your priority, but the same handful of ingredients decide it for most buyers:

  • Liquidity. The deepest order books mean you can buy large amounts without moving the price against you.
  • Fees. Trading and withdrawal costs compound over many purchases.
  • Security. Proof of Reserves, a published insurance/security fund, cold-storage custody and enforced 2FA.
  • Fiat on-ramps. Card, bank transfer, and (ideally) SEPA/Wire options at reasonable fees.
  • Availability. Some excellent exchanges are simply not accessible in your region.

How to Buy Bitcoin — Step by Step

  1. Pick your exchange (Binance and Bybit are the global default recommendations from this site).
  2. Create and verify your account. Standard KYC is required in 2026; set up an authenticator-app 2FA, and whitelist a withdrawal address.
  3. Fund the account via bank transfer, card, or peer-to-peer. Wire/SEPA usually has the lowest fees.
  4. Place your first order. A market order buys instantly; a limit order waits for your price. Start with a tiny market order to learn the screen.
  5. Withdraw to your own wallet for anything you are not actively trading — see our wallet guide to choose the right one.

Fees You Will Actually Pay

  • Trading fee: maker/taker % per order — lowest on Binance & Bybit; also lower if you hold the platform's coin or use pro-tier interfaces.
  • Deposit fee: usually free for bank transfer; card deposits carry ~1–3%.
  • Withdrawal fee: a fixed amount of Bitcoin per transfer, often flat; batch your withdrawals to avoid repeating it.
  • Spread: the invisible cost built into market orders — use limit orders to control it.

Security Checklist Before You Deposit

  • Enable 2FA with an authenticator app (never SMS-only).
  • Whitelist your withdrawal Bitcoin address(es) before first withdrawal.
  • Check the platform's Proof of Reserves page — a claim without one is a red flag.
  • Keep the bulk of your Bitcoin in self-custody, not on the exchange.
