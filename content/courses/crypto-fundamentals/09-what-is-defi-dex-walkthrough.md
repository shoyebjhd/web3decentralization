[DeFi](glossary/defi.md) — decentralized finance — is banking software that
runs on [smart contracts](glossary/smart-contract.md) instead of a bank. The
most beginner-friendly example is swapping tokens on a
[decentralized exchange (DEX)](glossary/decentralized-exchange.md). Let's walk
through one trade, from a beginner's point of view.

## The DEX trade, step by step

1. **Open a DEX** (like Uniswap) from a domain you verified yourself.
2. **Connect your wallet** — the DEX asks to "connect," it *reads* your address.
   It can't move funds yet.
3. **Approve** the token you're swapping — this grants the contract permission
   to spend exactly that token. Tighten limits when you can.
4. **Swap.** You'll see the rate, the [slippage](glossary/slippage.md)
   tolerance, and the [gas fee](glossary/gas.md). Confirm.
5. **Done.** The trade is on-chain, permanent, and yours — no middleman, no
   closing hours.

## What you didn't need

No account, no [KYC](glossary/kyc.md), no credit check, no withdrawal freeze.
Permissionless finance, in practice.

## What you're now responsible for

- **Smart-contract risk** — the code does exactly what the code says. Bugs and
  exploits happen to huge protocols.
- **Slippage & bots** — set conservative [slippage](glossary/slippage.md).
- **Your own approval hygiene** — never approve tokens you don't understand,
  and ideally use a fresh wallet for experiments.
- **[Impermanent loss](glossary/impermanent-loss.md)** if you're providing
  [liquidity](glossary/liquidity.md) — that's an advanced activity, not a
  beginner one.

## Try it safely (recommended)

Many chains have **testnets** with free test tokens
([learn more](glossary/testnet.md)). Practice a few swaps there before ever
putting real funds on a DEX — it's real DeFi, with nothing at risk.

> DeFi is the most powerful demonstration of "code instead of trust" — and the
> most demanding test of "trust the code, not the branding."
