The Nakamoto Coefficient is a number that answers one question: **how many
independent entities do you need to corrupt to take control of a network?**
It's named after Bitcoin's creator, Satoshi Nakamoto, and was popularized as a
way to measure decentralization.

The higher the number, the harder it is to compromise the network. A score of
1 would mean a single entity controls it; a score of 24 means you'd need 24
independent actors working together.

## Why it matters

Voter counts and "we have thousands of nodes!" claims are easy to spin. The
Nakamoto Coefficient cuts through that by asking the practical question — *how
much power is actually concentrated?* W3D's terminal calculates it per chain
across several dimensions so you can compare networks fairly.
