An address is the public identifier of a wallet — a long string of letters and
numbers (like `1A1zP1eP5QGefi2DMPTfTL5SLmv7DivfNa`, the first Bitcoin
address) that people use to send you funds. It's derived from your public key
and is safe to share.

## Why it matters

Addresses are not accounts with servers backing them — they're math-derived
identifiers. Anyone can generate one in seconds, free, and keep it
pseudonymous. Getting an address's characters wrong is irreversible, which is
why the network shows checksums and why you always double-check before
sending.
