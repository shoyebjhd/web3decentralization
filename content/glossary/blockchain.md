A blockchain is a shared, append-only digital ledger. Instead of one company
or bank holding the single record of truth, a **network of computers** each
keeps a copy of the same history. New entries ("blocks") are chained together
with cryptography, so past entries can't be quietly rewritten.

## Why it matters

Because no single party controls the record, blockchains enable things that
previously needed a trusted middleman: transferring value without a bank,
running code that no one can switch off, and proving facts without a central
authority. That shift in *who holds the record* is the heart of
decentralization.
