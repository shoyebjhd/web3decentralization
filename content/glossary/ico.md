An ICO (Initial Coin Offering) sells new tokens to the public before launch
to raise funds — crypto's 2017 craze, where whitepapers alone raised billions
and most buyers lost everything. Regulators worldwide later ruled many ICOs
were illegal unregistered securities sales.

## Why it matters

ICOs are history that rhymes: every cycle reinvents them (IEOs, IDOs, "fair"
mints, points). The pattern to recognize is unchanged — insiders price the
sale, retail provides exit liquidity, and "utility" does heavy lifting in the
pitch. Modern launchpads add KYC and lockups, but information asymmetry is
the product being sold as much as the token.
