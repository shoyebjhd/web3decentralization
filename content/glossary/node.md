A node is a computer that runs a blockchain's software and keeps a copy of the
ledger. Nodes validate transactions, relay data, and can propose new blocks
(when they're also validators/miners). The more independent nodes a network
has — across different owners and locations — the harder it is to control.

## Why it matters

"Nakamoto Coefficient" and infrastructure scores are really about nodes: how
many of them exist, who runs them, and how concentrated they are. A network
with thousands of nodes run by thousands of separate people is far harder to
compromise than one running on a handful of servers in a single building.
