---
title: "Address Poisoning"
related: [address, phishing, transaction]
---

Address poisoning is the scam of sending you tiny transactions from
look-alike addresses — same first and last characters as your real contacts —
so that when you copy "that address I used last time" from your history, you
grab the attacker's instead. The poison sits quietly in your history until
you slip.

## Why it matters

It defeats lazy verification: checking only first/last characters is exactly
what the attack mimics. Defenses are mechanical — use address books and ENS
names instead of history-copying, verify full addresses on hardware screens
for size, and treat any unexpected micro-transaction as hostile reconnaissance.
