Infrastructure & Outage Exposure

Cardano's standout is operator pluralism: ~3,000 geographically distributed stake pools give it a high Nakamoto Coefficient for infrastructure (24). Its exposure to hyperscalers is moderate (about 28% AWS), and the network is relatively resilient to cloud events thanks to that pool dispersion.

What the Score Means

  • Infrastructure (78): among the most distributed operator bases in crypto — hard for any party to compromise.
  • Capital (68): stake delegation is broad, though treasury entities (IOG, Emurgo) retain notable weight.
  • Software (60): the weak pillar — one dominant client (Cardano Node) means a severe bug could be systemic.

Cardano rose from ~38 (2017) to ~72 (2025) as Shelley decentralized staking shipped. See the timeline in the Terminal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Cardano decentralized?

Yes, largely — it prides itself on broad stake-pool distribution and now on-chain governance (Voltaire/CIP-1694). Its main weakness is a single dominant client.

How does Cardano's decentralization compare to Ethereum?

Cardano has stronger operator pluralism per validator but far thinner client diversity. Net composite it scores a bit below Ethereum (70.4 vs 80.8).

What is Voltaire?

Cardano's governance phase (CIP-1694) bringing on-chain community governance, raising its governance pillar as it matures.

Final Verdict

Cardano is a genuinely pluralist network with research-grade on-chain governance rolling out. Its single-client software pillar is the biggest risk and the main thing holding its composite below the leaders.
